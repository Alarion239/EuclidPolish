Power spectrum Std vs error Std vs brightness Calibration

Left: transfer function T(k) — does the ensemble mean carry the right amount of power per scale (T<1 over-smoothed, T>1 hallucinated power). Right: correlation with the truth r(k) per model + ensemble mean, and the model–model cross-correlation r̃(k) (grey dashed; median over member pairs). r̃ needs no HR, so where it tracks r(k) the inter-model agreement is a validated stand-in for “did we recover real structure?” on real Euclid images — where they split, the members agree with each other but not with the truth (shared hallucination).

Per-pixel cross-member std vs the actual error |ensemble mean − HR|. If disagreement is a usable error proxy the red binned median tracks the 0.674·σ guide (a calibrated Gaussian puts half the |error| mass below it). A median far above the guide at low σ = confidently-wrong pixels: the members agree on something that isn't there.

Where the disagreement lives: member std against the HR pixel value. Spread that rises with flux ~ photon-noise-driven (benign); excess spread at faint/sky pixels = hallucination on structure the LR doesn't constrain. The relative std (σ divided by brightness) falling with flux ⇒ bright structure is trustworthy.

Left: z = (mean − HR)/σ over all pixels vs the standard normal — σ(z) > 1 or heavy tails ⇒ the ensemble spread underestimates the true error (multiply σ by σ(z) to use it as an error bar). Right: per-field mean σ vs per-field RMSE — a high correlation means disagreement alone (no HR needed) can rank real fields by reconstruction quality.
